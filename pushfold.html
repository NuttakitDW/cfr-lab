If you remember one thing

Short stacks turn poker into arithmetic.

Below 15bb only two buttons are left: shove or fold. Every showdown's odds get worked out once and saved in a table. The solver never deals a hand. It multiplies ranges by tables until nobody wants to change.

Section 1 · The map

A tournament card room, four jobs

Picture the room behind a tournament table. Four people work there. Every file in the pushfold package belongs to one of them.

Oddsmaker
Knows every hand's chance against every other hand. Computed once, offline, kept in a table.
hands.py · oddsmaker.py
W, e2, eq3, pw
Floor
Knows who may act after what: shove, fold, call, overcall.
spot.py · floor.py
Spot, build()
Cashier
Pays out every ending: dead blinds, antes, side pots, capped stacks.
cashier.py · pricer.py
settle(), values()
Coach
Replays the table again and again, nudging each seat toward what paid more. The Auditor grades the work.
coach.py · auditor.py
solve(), audit()

Line counts: oddsmaker.py 231 · coach.py 145 · pricer.py 124 · floor.py 81 · spot.py 75 · cashier.py 74 · hands.py 64 · auditor.py 35. The whole engine is under 900 lines; the tables it reads are the heavy part.

Section 2 · How a spot gets solved

Every solve is the same four-step loop

1. DescribeSpot(stacks, ante): who sits where with how many big blinds.
→
2. Floorbuilds the tree: at most 128 decisions and 129 endings, even 9-handed.
→
3. Coachplays every hand class at once. The Cashier and the Oddsmaker price each ending.
→
4. Auditorsays "nobody gains more than 0.01bb". Print the chart.

Steps 3 and 4 repeat. The Auditor checks every 50 iterations by default (check_every). Heads-up is done in about 20ms, 9-handed in about a second.

What "warm start" means

A Library keeps every solved spot. A new spot starts from its nearest solved neighbour (same table size, closest stacks and ante).

seed the regrets from the neighbourkeep the average fresh

Only the regrets get seeded, so iteration 1 already plays the neighbour's chart. We also tried seeding the average strategy. It anchored the answer to the neighbour and took about 4x more iterations. Measured in Lab 7, regrets-only warm start halves the iterations at 2-5 players. At 7-9 players it made slow solves slower, because the nearest neighbour is often far away. A cold solve already needs only 10-30 iterations, so there is little left to save.

Section 3 · The things that trip people up

Five things to remember

169 is not 169 equal hands.

AA has 6 combos, AKs 4, AKo 12. Weight by combos or the ranges come out wrong. Lab 4 drops the weights: the SB's heads-up shove range shrinks from 58.6% to 50.4%.

Blockers shrink matchups. Use them heads-up.

AA vs AKs is 12 combo pairs, not 24. The W matrix makes heads-up exact. At 3+ seats we tried giving each seat its own blockers. The EVs stopped summing to zero (+0.21bb at 9-handed) and landed further from real deals than dealing opponents independently. So multiway deals opponents independently. Card removal still lives inside the equity tables.

Side pots need finish order, not equity.

The short stack can only win the main pot. The side pot goes to the better of the other two hands. In Lab 2, KK's share of a side pot against QQ is 82.0%, not the 53.7% you get by rescaling 3-way equity.

Deep stacks don't matter past the effective stack.

100bb against 10bb plays exactly like 10bb against 10bb. Chips nobody can match come straight back. The Cashier gets this for free by layering pots by contribution.

"Strategy stopped changing" does not mean solved.

Measure exploitability: the most any one seat could gain by changing alone. Lab 3's chart still shows mixed hands at 0.0006bb. With 3 or more players there can be more than one equilibrium, and CFR has no guarantee to converge, so the Auditor is the only judge.

LAB 1

Count the combos

5 min

The Oddsmaker's first job is knowing how many real two-card hands hide behind each of the 169 labels. This lab uses plain Python, no package. The 169 classes are the suit symmetry from the isomorphism page.

lab1.py
# Lab 1: count the combos. The Oddsmaker's first job is knowing how many
# real two-card hands hide behind each of the 169 labels.
import itertools


RANKS = "23456789TJQKA"
deck = range(52)                         # card = rank * 4 + suit


def label(a, b):
    hi, lo = max(a // 4, b // 4), min(a // 4, b // 4)
    if hi == lo:
        return RANKS[hi] * 2
    return RANKS[hi] + RANKS[lo] + ("s" if a % 4 == b % 4 else "o")


combos = list(itertools.combinations(deck, 2))
by_class = {}
for c in combos:
    by_class.setdefault(label(*c), []).append(c)

print("classes:", len(by_class), " combos:", len(combos))
print("AA", len(by_class["AA"]), " AKs", len(by_class["AKs"]), " AKo", len(by_class["AKo"]))


def W(x, y):
    """Combo pairs (one from x, one from y) that share no card."""
    return sum(1 for p in by_class[x] for q in by_class[y] if not set(p) & set(q))


for x, y in [("AA", "AKs"), ("AA", "AA"), ("KK", "AKo")]:
    naive = len(by_class[x]) * len(by_class[y])
    print(f"W[{x}][{y}] = {W(x, y):3}   naive {naive}")
Predict firstHow many AA-vs-AKs combo pairs can actually be dealt together? The naive answer is 6 x 4 = 24.
Check yourself

12. Every AKs combo uses one ace. Only C(3,2) = 3 AA combos avoid that ace, so 4 x 3 = 12. And AA vs AA is 6: each AA combo has exactly one AA combo it shares no card with.

classes: 169  combos: 1326
AA 6  AKs 4  AKo 12
W[AA][AKs] =  12   naive 24
W[AA][AA] =   6   naive 36
W[KK][AKo] =  36   naive 72

The full 169 x 169 version of W is one matrix product in hands.py. Each row sums to combos x 1225, the hands left for one opponent.

Try it: print W("KK", "AKs") too. KK against any AK is 12 + 36 = 48 pairs, not 64.

Hire the Oddsmaker

LAB 4

Break it on purpose

10 min

Two bugs that are easy to write. First, forget combo weights. Second, collect antes but never put them in the pot.

lab4.py
# Lab 4: break it on purpose, twice.
import numpy as np

from pushfold import auditor, cashier, coach, hands, pricer
from pushfold.spot import Spot

spot = Spot(stacks=(10, 10))
honest = coach.solve(spot)
print(f"honest       SB shoves {honest.range_pct(0):.1%}")

# Break 1: pretend all 169 classes are equally likely (AA as common as AKo).
real_prior, real_m = hands.PRIOR, hands.M
hands.PRIOR = np.full(169, 1 / 169)
hands.M = np.full((169, 169), 1 / 169)
pricer._two_way.cache_clear()
flat = coach.solve(spot)
share = real_prior @ flat.strategy[0][:, 1]          # measured on the real deck
print(f"equal weight SB shoves {share:.1%}")
hands.PRIOR, hands.M = real_prior, real_m
pricer._two_way.cache_clear()

# Break 2: collect antes but forget to put them in the pot.
real_settle = cashier.settle


def leaky(spot, jammers):
    s = real_settle(spot, jammers)
    dead = sum(spot.antes)
    if s.layers:                                     # showdown: main pot is short
        first = cashier.Layer(s.layers[0].amount - dead, s.layers[0].eligible)
        return cashier.Settlement(s.fixed, (first,) + s.layers[1:])
    fixed = s.fixed.copy()                           # fold: the winner gets less
    fixed[np.argmax(fixed)] -= dead
    return cashier.Settlement(fixed, s.layers)


with_ante = Spot(stacks=(10, 10), ante=0.125)
for name, settle in (("real", real_settle), ("leaky", leaky)):
    cashier.settle = settle
    result = coach.solve(with_ante)
    ev = auditor.audit(result.tree, result.strategy).ev
    print(f"{name:5} cashier  EV SB {ev[0]:+.4f}  BB {ev[1]:+.4f}  sum {ev.sum():+.4f}")
cashier.settle = real_settle
Predict firstIf every class is equally likely (AA as common as AKo), does the SB shove wider or tighter?
Check yourself

Tighter: 50.4% instead of 58.6%. Equal weights make suited hands about 2x too common, pairs 1.3x too common, and offsuit hands too rare. The deck looks stronger than it is, so shoving looks worse.

honest       SB shoves 58.6%
equal weight SB shoves 50.4%
real  cashier  EV SB -0.0498  BB +0.0498  sum +0.0000
leaky cashier  EV SB -0.1678  BB -0.0822  sum -0.2500

The leaky Cashier makes 0.25bb vanish every hand: exactly the two antes. Summing EVs is the cheapest test you can write, and it catches this bug at once.

Try it: set the ante to 0.25. The leak doubles to -0.5000.

LAB 7

1,000 random tables

15 min (8 min of it running)

The at-scale test: random table size, stacks, ante type. Every solve must pass the Auditor and stay zero-sum. The Library warm-starts each spot from its nearest solved neighbour.

lab7.py
# Lab 7: 1,000 random tables. 2-9 players, stacks 1-15bb, random antes.
# Every solve must pass the Auditor and stay zero-sum.
import time

import numpy as np

from pushfold import auditor, coach
from pushfold.spot import Spot

rng = np.random.default_rng(2026)
library = coach.Library()          # set to None to turn warm start off
seconds, iters = {}, {}
began = time.perf_counter()

for _ in range(1000):
    n = int(rng.integers(2, 10))
    mode = str(rng.choice(["each", "bb"]))
    ante = float(rng.choice([0, 0.1, 0.125])) if mode == "each" else 1.0
    spot = None
    while spot is None:            # redraw until every stack covers its blind + ante
        try:
            spot = Spot(tuple(rng.uniform(1, 15, n).round(1)), ante=ante, ante_mode=mode)
        except ValueError:
            pass
    r = coach.solve(spot, check_every=10, library=library)
    report = auditor.audit(r.tree, r.strategy)
    assert r.exploitability < 0.01, (spot, r.exploitability)
    assert abs(report.ev.sum()) < 1e-8, (spot, report.ev.sum())
    seconds.setdefault(n, []).append(r.seconds)
    iters.setdefault(n, []).append(r.iterations)

print(f"1000 tables, every one < 0.01bb exploitable and zero-sum, {time.perf_counter() - began:.0f}s total")
print(" n  tables  iters p50   p50 s   p95 s")
for n in sorted(seconds):
    s = np.array(seconds[n])
    print(f"{n:>2}  {len(s):>6}  {np.median(iters[n]):>9.0f}  {np.median(s):6.2f}  {np.percentile(s, 95):6.2f}")

Section 5 · Where it fits

What this solver is for, and what it is not

Use it for

  • Tournament spots at 15bb or less, in chip EV
  • Any table size from heads-up to 9-handed
  • Charts on demand for any stacks and antes, per ante or BB ante
  • A baseline for a bot or a voice coach to quote

It is not

  • ICM ($EV). That is the next step: only the Cashier's payout changes
  • Deeper-stack play. That needs min-raises and limps, then postflop
  • Exploitative play against real opponents
  • A model of 4-way all-ins. Once 3 players are all in, everyone left must fold

Recall cards: answer out loud, then open

What is the one sentence?

Short stacks turn poker into arithmetic. Only shove or fold remain, showdown odds are precomputed tables, and the solver multiplies ranges by tables until nobody wants to change.

Name the four jobs in the card room.

Oddsmaker (equity tables), Floor (who may act after what), Cashier (payouts, side pots, capped stacks), Coach (CFR+, graded by the Auditor).

How many combos: AA, AKs, AKo, and in total?

6, 4, 12, and 1326 across 169 classes.

Why is AA vs AKs 12 combo pairs and not 24?

Each AKs combo uses one ace, leaving only C(3,2) = 3 AA combos. 4 x 3 = 12. That is the W matrix.

Why isn't equity enough for a 3-way all-in with unequal stacks?

The short stack only plays for the main pot. The side pot goes to the better of the other two hands, which needs finish order (pw), not the 3-way equity split.

What does 100bb vs 10bb play like, and why?

Like 10bb vs 10bb. Chips nobody can match are returned, so pots are layered by contribution and capped at the effective stack.

Why full-width CFR+ here instead of MCCFR?

The tree is tiny (at most 128 decisions) and each iteration is a few matrix products over all 169 hands. Sampling would only add noise.

What is the stop rule, and why not "strategy stopped changing"?

Stop when the Auditor finds no seat can gain more than 0.01bb per hand. A strategy can sit still and still be exploitable, and multiway CFR has no convergence guarantee.

What invariant does every solved spot satisfy?

The EVs of all seats sum to zero, blinds and antes included (to 1e-8 in Lab 7). A leak like Lab 4's shows up at once.

What single thing changes to make this an ICM solver?

The terminal payout. The Cashier's chip results become prize money through the payout structure. The Floor, Oddsmaker and Coach stay the same.

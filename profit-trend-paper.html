The Trend in Firm Profitability

Akbas, Jiang & Koch 2017 — the slope of profits, not the level Back to index
Authors Akbas · Jiang · Koch
Published The Accounting Review 92(5)
Sample 1977 – 2012
Hedge return 0.83% / month
Method Decile sorts + Fama–MacBeth
The single insight

Two firms can be equally profitable today and have opposite futures. The market prices the level and ignores the slope.

Gross profitability — the Novy-Marx workhorse — asks how profitable is this firm? Akbas, Jiang and Koch ask a second question off the same eight quarters of data: which way is it heading? The recent trajectory of profits predicts future profits, predicts next quarter's earnings surprise, predicts analysts' forecast errors for a full year — and predicts returns. None of that is absorbed by the level of profitability or by earnings momentum.

Section 1 · The result in one picture

Same average profitability, opposite slope

Both firms below post exactly the same eight-quarter average gross profitability. A level sort puts them in the same decile. The trend sort puts them at opposite ends of the book — and the spread between them is where the return lives.

positive profit trend — buy side negative profit trend — sell side identical level, so a level sort cannot separate them

The two paths in this illustration are mirror images of each other, which is the cleanest way to hold the level fixed. Real deciles are not that tidy — the paper's contribution is showing the slope still carries information after the level is controlled for.

Section 2 · What each side is

The trajectory tells you what the level cannot

Long leg · rising profits

Improving firms stay improving

positive slope → higher future profitability → + future return

Why: profitability is persistent in direction, not just in level. A firm whose margins have climbed for two years usually keeps climbing for at least another quarter. Investors extrapolate the current level and underweight the direction, so the good news keeps arriving as a surprise.

Short leg · falling profits

Deteriorating firms keep deteriorating

negative slope → lower future profitability → − future return

Why: a firm still showing a respectable profitability level while its slope rolls over looks fine on a screen. The level sort keeps buying it. The trend sort is already short, and gets paid as the deterioration shows up in reported numbers.

The two effects are roughly symmetric: the paper documents that both increasing and decreasing drifts in the last eight quarters of gross profitability move subsequent prices.

Section 3 · The two measures

Both come out of the same eight-quarter window

GP

Quarterly gross profitability

(Revenue − COGS) / Total Assets

Novy-Marx's measure, computed quarter by quarter. The rawest line on the income statement, deflated by assets rather than by price so it stays independent of past returns.

LEVEL

Profitability level

mean(GP over past 8 quarters)

The conventional profitability signal. Averaging over two years smooths quarterly noise and seasonality — this is the control variable the trend must beat.

TREND

Profitability trend

slope of GP on a time trend, 8 qtrs

Regress the firm's eight quarterly GP observations on a time trend, quarterly seasonal dummies and a constant. The time-trend coefficient is the signal. Seasonal dummies keep a Q4-heavy business from reading as a trend.

SUE

Earnings momentum (the rival)

standardised earnings surprise

The obvious competing explanation: maybe the profit trend is just post-earnings-announcement drift wearing a hat. The paper controls for it and the trend survives.

The design point: level and trend are the intercept and the slope of the same eight-quarter fit. Nothing extra is being asked of the data — the second moment was always there, unused.

Section 4 · The method

How the portfolios are built

STEP 01
Build the panel
CRSP + Compustat quarterly, US common stocks, January 1977 through December 2012.
STEP 02
Fit each firm-quarter
Regress the last 8 quarters of GP on a time trend plus seasonal dummies. Keep the slope.
STEP 03
Sort into deciles
Each month, rank on the trend. Long the top decile, short the bottom, equal-weighted.
STEP 04
Price the spread
Run the H−L series through the three- and four-factor models; confirm with Fama–MacBeth cross-sectional regressions.

Section 5 · The key numbers

What the trend-sorted spread earned, gross

Measure Value What it means
H−L raw return 0.83% / mo Equal-weighted next-month return, long the highest-trend decile and short the lowest.
3-factor alpha 0.85% / mo Against market, size and book-to-market. Alpha slightly exceeds the raw return — the spread is not a repackaged value or size tilt.
4-factor alpha 0.64% / mo Adding momentum eats about a quarter of it. Some of the profit trend is momentum-adjacent, but most of it is not.
Small-cap leg 0.89% / mo Where most of the effect lives — the usual home of accounting anomalies, and the usual home of trading costs.
Large-cap leg 0.34% / mo Still positive, and this is the number that matters for anything running real size.

All figures are gross — before commissions, spreads, shorting costs and the data cost of building the panel. The large-minus-small gap is the honest read on capacity.

Section 6 · The mechanism

Underreaction, and specifically not overreaction

An anomaly paper is only interesting if it says why. This one runs the standard behavioural fork — did investors underreact to good information, or overreact to noise? — and lands firmly on the first branch.

Test 1 — does the trend predict the fundamentals? Yes. The profit trend predicts future profitability itself, which is the precondition for any of this to be information rather than a statistical artefact.
Test 2 — does it predict the surprise? Yes. The trend predicts the earnings surprise one quarter later. If the market had already priced the trajectory, the next quarter's number would not be a surprise in the direction the trend pointed.
Test 3 — do the professionals miss it too? Yes. The trend predicts sell-side analyst forecast errors over the following twelve months — the sophisticated end of the market is systematically on the wrong side of the trajectory, not just retail.

The counter-test comes back empty: the authors find no evidence of investor overreaction, and the returns are not explained by well-known risk factors. So the story is not "trend-followers bid these up and it reverses." It is "the information was sitting in the last eight quarterly filings and nobody extracted the slope."

Section 7 · The horse race

Four things the profit trend is not

  • Not the profitability level. The trend survives controlling for the eight-quarter average GP — which is the whole point, since both are estimated on the same window.
  • Not earnings momentum. Controlling for standardised earnings surprise does not subsume it. The slope of gross profit and the shock to reported earnings are different objects.
  • Not price momentum. The four-factor alpha of 0.64% is smaller than the three-factor alpha but stays economically large, so momentum absorbs part and not most.
  • Not a risk premium. The returns are not explained by the standard factor models, and the underreaction evidence gives a positive mispricing story rather than an unexplained residual.

Section 8 · Before you trade it

What the paper does not promise

Gross, not net

Every return above is before trading frictions. A monthly-rebalanced decile spread on small caps is exactly the shape of strategy that loses most of its edge to costs.

The short leg has to be borrowable

The bottom decile of a small-cap profitability screen is not reliably available to short, and not cheaply.

Sample ends in 2012

The published sample stops the year the working paper started circulating. Anything you conclude about the last decade is out of sample, and profitability signals in particular have been heavily mined since.

Eight quarters is a choice

The window length, the seasonal dummies and the linear functional form are all researcher degrees of freedom. A trend measure is only as robust as its insensitivity to those, which is worth checking before committing capital.

Section 9 · Bottom line

Three things to remember

01

Every level signal has a hidden slope signal

If you already compute a fundamental ratio over a rolling window, you already have the trend for free — you are throwing away the regression's slope and keeping only its mean.

02

The evidence for mispricing is the forecast error, not the return

The strongest part of the paper is not the 0.83%. It is that the trend predicts the next earnings surprise and a year of analyst errors — direct evidence that the information was not in the price.

03

Read the size split before you get excited

0.89% small versus 0.34% large is the difference between a paper result and a deployable one. The honest version of this signal is the large-cap number, net of costs.
